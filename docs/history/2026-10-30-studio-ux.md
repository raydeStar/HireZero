# Studio look and layered transparency (UX round 10)

The owner asked for the workspace to look more like the personal agents Muse (Meta) and Dot (OpenAI's dots), to feel
more enterprise, and to be more useful. Then they added: "full transparency on what it's doing", with the ability to
drill in and to see what is going on when it matters.

Both references are conversation-first. They report progress in plain sentences ("Ready for review"), show what the
agent is working on at a glance, and keep the technical record one layer down: Muse's three-layer status and audit
trail, and the review queue and permissions in dots. HireZero already kept the full record (receipts, costs,
provenance, audit log), but put it either up front or out of sight. This round arranges it in three layers and hides
nothing.

## Three layers of status

1. **Always visible.**
   - A live strip under the top bar on every page names who is working on what, and for how long, with a link to watch.
     When the cockpit is out of view, it shows what needs you instead.
   - The sidebar's line under each employee now says what they are doing ("Working on …", "2 tasks wait for your
     review", "Idle") instead of only "Active". Their dot pulses while they work.
   - Home opens with a date, a greeting, today's summary and live counts (Needs you, Drafts ready, Working, Up next).
     The counts that have a destination open it.
2. **What happened.** Every piece of work opens with a "What happened" trail. It lists the saved events for the task,
   its measured attempts and its results, in order, with the time, who took the step and the offset from the first
   step. It opens on its own while work is running. The internal HireZero plan's saved events give that trail: assigned,
   job prepared, one request authorized, draft ready 22 seconds later.
3. **The record.** Receipts, model usage, costs, sources and the audit log are unchanged and sit below the trail.

Nothing that informs a decision was hidden:
- the run terms and the consent before Generate are unchanged;
- the reply settings under the composer stay on screen as compact chips;
- approvals happen where they did, with the same checks.

## Look and layout

- A new final style layer, `web/src/company/studio.css`, covers the following:
  - a warmer neutral palette in light and dark;
  - near-black primary actions, with the accent kept for selection and attention;
  - a slightly larger type scale;
  - one calm surface for the rail, conversations and cockpit, beside a clean main sheet;
  - round avatars with a steady colour per employee;
  - a segmented Chat | Work switch;
  - a readable draft column with sticky copy controls;
  - quiet uppercase labels in the cockpit, with a pulse beside live work.
- Chat and an open piece of work now sit side by side from 1600 pixels wide, up from 1440. At 1440 pixels with the
  cockpit docked, both columns had been about 200 pixels wide. Below 1600 pixels the work replaces the chat, with a way
  back, as [ADR 0040](../adr/0040-the-muse-layout.md) already provides for narrower screens.
- The goal form is a card, and on phones a result's status chip wraps below its title.
