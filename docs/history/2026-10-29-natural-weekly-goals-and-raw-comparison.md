# Natural weekly goals and raw comparison

The next installed Home evaluation used a fresh fictional two-person content agency, one business
sentence and a goal beginning “Create a week's LinkedIn content: three ready-to-post posts…”. The
owner wanted three posts that help consultants use client questions, show the agency's service,
and include a manageable routine and conversation signal. No website, prior draft or feedback was
provided. Both requests used native Luna through Codex OAuth, confirmed high effort, policy v3,
a 6,000-token output target, a 90-second local deadline and one intended existing credit per request.
These bounds remain soft. Neither request published content or performed external actions.

## Finding and correction

Home selected the general direct-asset skill for the first request. Its weekly detector recognized
“weekly content plan” but missed “a week's LinkedIn content” and hyphenated modifiers such as
“ready-to-post”. The raw result contained three grounded posts and a natural service connection, but
their advice overlapped and lacked a worked example. Its routine told the owner to draft another
post instead of helping use the three already supplied. This first result is not ready unedited by
assistant assessment; it remains saved as the first-run evidence.

Marketing's role-owned setup now recognizes explicit requests for a week's content or posts,
including named channels and “one week of” phrasing. The three-post detector also accepts hyphenated
modifiers. Regression checks preserve direct-asset requests that merely discuss weekly content,
newsletter requests and explicitly excluded weekly work. No role instructions, model defaults or
approval policy changed.

## Later comparison, not replacement acceptance

After updating the installed web files, the same business and goal selected the weekly-plan skill
on Home. One separately counted comparison ran in a fresh private conversation. Saved packets have
the same approved brief digest and no prior history, evidence or notes; the new skill and its
assignment instructions are the intended difference.

The comparison has more distinct post purposes, a concrete hypothetical feedback example, and a
50-minute routine covering publishing, comment replies and tallying. It still needs edits:

- The same service sentence appears in all three posts.
- Two posts ask commenters to identify the post they are already commenting on; the learning
  section repeats that unnecessary attribution step.
- The weak and inconclusive decision branches overlap.

This is a stronger candidate, not a raw-usefulness pass or proof of a consistent model improvement.
One comparison cannot separate the skill's effect from run-to-run variation. The next quality work
should address channel-appropriate attribution and repetitive service copy, then use fresh cases.
There were no assistant-authored editorial artifacts, revision requests or automatic retries.
