# UX audit and design direction (2026-10-20)

The owner handed the user experience over for an enterprise-quality redesign, after the Muse layout returned
([ADR 0040](../adr/0040-the-muse-layout.md)). This note records what a walk through every screen found, at 1440×900
with fictional data, the direction for fixing it, and the order of the work.

## What the walk found

The pattern behind most findings: **the app shows its safety machinery before the job.** Allowances, native turns,
evidence and authority statements come first, and what the owner came to do comes second. The same information also
appears in several places at once.

1. **The composer buries the message.** In an employee chat, these all stack between the conversation and the message
   box, taking about half the screen:
   - Work type;
   - Message action;
   - Intended reply credit allowance;
   - a paragraph about native turns and soft stops;
   - Actions;
   - Attach approved evidence.
2. **Three layers of header.** Above every chat sit:
   - the top bar;
   - a block with the employee's responsibility, a capability sentence, a conversation picker and "New conversation";
   - a "Conversation context and employee settings" disclosure.
3. **The home is a chat you cannot chat in.** "Company conversation" opens with a legal paragraph ("The human owner
   retains final authority…"), and its composer defaults to "Reply as: Save owner note only".
4. **The sidebar is uneven:**
   - employee entries are indented differently from one another;
   - "Hire or manage employees" and "Today, budgets, members and settings" look like body text;
   - an authority disclaimer sits between them.
5. **People opens on a hiring form,** not on the team.
6. **Today repeats the cockpit,** with a full-width priority dropdown and a full-width Snooze button under every item.
7. **Names that need translating:**
   - "Company" in the rail next to "Company conversation";
   - "Board" for meetings;
   - "Approve content" for approving a result.
8. **Visual rhythm:**
   - long reports dominate the chat;
   - there are many small font sizes;
   - collapsed `<details>` arrows are used as the main way to reveal things.

## Direction

- **Job first, safety one step away.** The message box and its send button come first. Options sit in one compact row
  under it. The explanation of how a reply is authorized is one click away, never in the way. Every safety check
  stays exactly where it is enforced: on the server and on the job card.
- **One home for each thing:**
  - what needs you lives in the cockpit;
  - the day's history lives in Today;
  - settings live in the Company hub;
  - the team lives in People.
- **Calm, plain language.** No legal paragraphs in headers. Short labels and verbs that say what happens: "Approve",
  "Ask for changes", "Prepare a reply".
- **Consistent, scannable lists:**
  - the same row anatomy (avatar or icon, title, one muted line, status) in the sidebar, the cockpit, the board and
    People;
  - buttons look like buttons;
  - one indentation rule.
- **Progressive disclosure through panels,** not long pages: actions and evidence open as panels above the composer,
  and hiring opens from a button.

## Order of work

1. **Conversation:**
   - a compact composer, with options in one row and Actions and Evidence as panels;
   - a one-line conversation header;
   - a calm empty state.
2. **Shell:**
   - a consistent sidebar, with initials, a status dot and clear Hire and Settings buttons;
   - "Board" renamed "Meetings".
3. **People:** the team first, hiring from a button.
4. **Today:** compact rows with inline priority and snooze.
5. **Next rounds:**
   - a real Home overview, with the team's status and recent results;
   - reports shown as summaries with "show full report";
   - a type scale and spacing pass;
   - keyboard shortcuts;
   - light-theme review.

## Done so far

- **Round 1 (feature/ux-round-1):**
  - the compact composer and the one-line conversation header;
  - the sidebar's rows and foot actions, and "Meetings" in the rail;
  - People showing the team first;
  - compact Today rows.
- **Round 2 (feature/ux-round-2):**
  - **Home is the landing page.** It has tiles (Needs you, which opens the first item; In progress; Up next; Done;
    Spent to date), team cards whose status line comes from real results needing review, and recent results with
    state chips, each opening its work window.
  - **Long reports show their opening lines** with a fade and "Show full report". The full text stays in the page.
  - **The review buttons say "Approve result" and "Ask for changes".**
- **Round 3 (feature/ux-round-3):**
  - **One type scale.** All 75 font sizes in the company styles use the Muse tokens, in place of 15 ad hoc sizes.
  - **One palette.** About thirty dark-only literal colours became theme tokens. This removes the teal tint, makes
    primary buttons the same blue accent as everything else, and makes the light theme correct.
  - **A theme switch** (System, Light, Dark) at the foot of the sidebar. System follows the operating system as it
    changes.
  - **Keyboard shortcuts.** `?` shows them all. `/` writes a message. `g` followed by h, c, p, s, l or m goes to a
    page. `w` switches Chat and Work. `.` shows or hides the cockpit. Esc closes the open panel or work. None fire
    while typing.
  - **Sharing an approved result** is folded behind "Share with another employee" instead of an open form in the
    conversation.
- **Round 4 (feature/ux-round-4):**
  - **Fixed a round 1 regression:** on phones the conversation list showed as an open drawer over every page. It is
    closed again until opened, and the Muse smoke now checks this.
  - **Company hub panels** carry their summary as a section header ("Members (1)", "Audit log: intact, 7 entries")
    instead of a collapsible inside a tab.
  - **Budgets** is a styled table: quiet headers, right-aligned numbers, inline Change.
  - **Integrations** shows "Set up" forms first, then "Other integrations" as compact rows with a status dot. An
    integration with a form is no longer listed twice.
  - **Plans and Meetings** without a CEO show one clear state ("Hire a CEO") instead of a form that cannot work.
    Meetings is named the same in the rail and the title, and participant checkboxes sit beside their names.
  - **The phone tab bar** fits all six places.
- **Round 5 (feature/ux-round-5).** A walk through the most consequential flow, an ad change from reading to
  "Publish once":
  - **The job card reads in order:** the request, the authorization, the result, publication, then Complete or
    Cancel. Before this, the publication box ("approve the result above") and the finishing buttons came before the
    result.
  - **The exact call is shown separately.** A publishing reading's machine-readable `Call: {json}` line is shown
    apart from the reading, formatted, under "Exact call". The approved text and its digest are unchanged
    (ADR 0017). This closes the last part of walkthrough finding 7.
  - **One copy of the open work.** While a job is open in the work window, the chat shows a placeholder for it,
    not a second copy that loads its state separately and can disagree with the window.
  - The whole chain (reading, approval, proposal, approval of the exact call, publish once) was walked end to end
    against the fictional ads account.
- **Round 6 (feature/ux-round-6).** A walk through the first run, from an empty company through the hiring guide:
  - **Getting started on Home.** Four steps lead from an empty company to a first approved result: hire, finish
    onboarding, ask for work, approve a result. Each is ticked from real data, the next one carries its button, and
    the list disappears when all four are done.
  - **An unfinished hire is a "Needs you" item** ("Marketing partner: finish onboarding"), unless its department
    already has draft context waiting, which is its own item. Opening it resumes the hiring guide at the right step.
  - **Checked and fine:** the hiring guide's steps and defaults; keyboard focus rings (the accent outline on
    `:focus-visible`); muted text contrast of about 6:1 in both themes.
- **Round 7 (feature/ux-round-7).** A walk through the core chat interaction: ask, prepare a reply, review its
  context, authorize, read the answer.
  - **The waiting reply card is compact:** what it sends and its limit on one line, one "Review reply and context"
    button, the authorization checkbox beside a short statement, and Send. The second "…to unlock" button that did
    the same thing is gone. While the box is locked, a hint under it says to review first.
  - **The explanations moved into Safety details:** what Send authorizes, and that later messages wait for a new
    turn. Nothing about the authorization changed: the same review gate, statement of soft limits, and exact-packet
    binding apply.
  - **Readable review panel:** conversation history and evidence are lists, not raw JSON. The complete sealed
    context is still one click away.
  - **A finished turn is one quiet line** between the question and the answer. The "informational reply" disclaimer
    on every AI message moved into "How replies work".
  - The walkthrough smoke follows the single Review button and the hint.

## Round 8: real use

- Work cards show what was asked, in one line, and when the work last changed.
- The cockpit shows how long running work has run (live), and when other rows changed. Times come from the event
  log through a new `taskTimes` map, since tasks carry no times of their own.
- Home opens with one sentence on the day: started, finished, and what waits on you.
- Needs you names knowledge drafts ("Marketing: approve Chip's context") and opens them in the hire's guide.
- Departments edits context from its latest version, with labelled fields and Fill from sources; Company facts
  (ADR 0043) has the same editor.
